If your site looks like it was built in 2005, it's a problem. Your website is often the first impression that potential customers have of your business. And if your website is outdated, difficult to use, or slow to load, you could be losing customers without even knowing it.

In this article, we'll take a look at 5 signs that your website is actively losing you customers.

## 1. Your Website is Not Mobile-Friendly

In today's mobile-first world, it's essential that your website is mobile-friendly. If your website is not optimized for mobile devices, you're providing a poor user experience for a large portion of your audience. This can lead to a high bounce rate, low conversion rates, and a negative perception of your brand.

## 2. Your Website is Slow to Load

No one likes a slow website. In fact, studies have shown that if a website takes more than 3 seconds to load, a significant portion of users will abandon it. A slow website can be caused by a number of factors, including large images, bloated code, and a slow web host.

## 3. Your Website is Difficult to Navigate

If your website is difficult to navigate, users will quickly become frustrated and leave. Your website should have a clear and intuitive navigation structure that makes it easy for users to find what they're looking for.

> "Don't make me think." - *Steve Krug, Author of "Don't Make Me Think: A Common Sense Approach to Web Usability"*

## 4. Your Website Has a Poor User Experience (UX)

User experience (UX) is all about how a user feels when they're interacting with your website. A good UX is one that is easy, intuitive, and enjoyable. A poor UX, on the other hand, is one that is confusing, frustrating, and annoying.

A poor UX can be caused by a number of factors, including a cluttered layout, confusing navigation, and a lack of clear calls to action.

## 5. Your Website is Not Secure

If your website is not secure, you're putting your customers' data at risk. A non-secure website is one that does not use HTTPS, which is a protocol that encrypts the data that is sent between a user's browser and your website.

If your website is not secure, Google will display a "Not Secure" warning in the address bar, which can scare away potential customers.
